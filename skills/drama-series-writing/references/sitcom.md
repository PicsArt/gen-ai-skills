# Sitcom and mockumentary

Build a relationship through one concrete job. For a new pilot or rejected incident, consider two
incidents privately and choose the one whose consequence reveals the people with least staging.
A stranger should understand what happens here, why these people are together and why to return.

## A playable incident

Give two people different intentions around one request, assignment, ritual or deadline. Each
understands something and misjudges something. Turn a trait into a consequential choice. Someone
who wants to appear decisive may quietly need help; someone capable may want inclusion more than
efficiency. Contradiction can be funnier than incompetence.

| Beat | Craft check |
|---|---|
| Want | One concrete task, two intentions in conflict |
| Choice | A lead changes the task or relationship by acting |
| Turn | The choice overturns an expectation planted earlier |
| Payoff | The local incident lands through an observable consequence |
| Continuity delta | A responsibility, relationship or specific consequence survives |

Read only the spoken lines and observable actions. If explanatory notes carry the joke, rewrite.
Check literal cause and consequence. A reaction shows a feeling about an event; it cannot supply
the event. A compromise or supportive lesson alone is not a comic reversal. Rotate who surprises
whom rather than ending every episode with one person insulting another who looks at camera.

## Performance and timing

Write ordinary, specific speech. Give each line an intention toward the partner: reassure,
conceal, bargain, recruit or stall. Let pauses change intention. Reserve silent reaction time
before filling the remaining time with words. Do not repair an overflowing script by relabeling
seconds or demanding faster delivery. Remove lines that explain the joke.

Keep breathing, eyeline changes and motivated gestures. A look to camera can expose a private
admission, affection or pride as well as embarrassment. A talking-head aside may contradict a
visible choice; it should not explain an event the episode failed to show.

## Physical mechanism

Spend complexity on one primary interaction. Show cause, contact or change, and consequence.
A transfer needs approach, contact, release and settled ownership. Inspect literal object counts
and physical causality. A late shot of an object at its destination does not prove the transfer.
Keep optional handling simple; preserve any action the user required.

Cuts can separate setup, interaction and consequence while keeping geography, ownership, sound
and direction. If the brief requires an uninterrupted take, simplify within that take. Do not
conceal an impossible action with an occlusion jump or frozen ending. Inspect required native
artwork and lettering literally in stills and motion; a blank surface is not a branding repair.

## Variety across a season

Reuse locked cast, voices, wardrobe/material rules and a small location set. Rotate pairings,
wants, physical actions and consequences. Advance what happened before instead of replaying the
same request. Resolve the local incident. A serial comedy may carry a larger unresolved question;
a resolved comedy with no successor ends settled and needs no cliffhanger.

For 20+ episodes, outline every distinct incident with want, complication, payoff and continuity
delta. Fully script only the next few. Prove the pilot end to end, then test another pairing and
physical action before assuming the method generalizes. Keep each episode's review evidence and
actual master link. Scripts and quotes are not finished episodes.
