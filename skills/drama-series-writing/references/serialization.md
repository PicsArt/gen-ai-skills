# Serialization

Read against the accepted episode ledger before writing the next outline. Establish whether this
episode has a successor. The seam, reveal ration, rupture duration and cliffhanger rotation apply
to serial episodes. A terminal pilot, standalone, resolved comedy or finale pays its debts instead.

## Open on the consequence

The first 3 s earn a new question. Show what the previous ending is doing to someone, without
repeating it or answering it immediately. The previous cliffhanger's answer lands no earlier
than the second half, and opens a larger problem when another episode follows. A Reveal already
shown to the audience stays known; delay the unresolved question it raised, not the fact itself.

## Spend the bank slowly

Use at most one reveal per serial episode. The open-thread ledger never net-decreases before
the finale: close a thread only while opening at least one. Record thread ids, plants, owners,
planned payoff and actual closure. Count open threads before and after; do not hide closures by
renaming the same question. A no-successor episode is also terminal and may spend the ledger down.

Let a rupture last at least three episodes when the remaining run allows it. Coercion does not
erase betrayal. Forgiveness is a payoff, not an automatic reaction. Honor a sacrifice rather than
refunding it. Plan the final healing or reckoning within the commissioned run.

## Show a state change

| Test | Required change |
|---|---|
| Initiative | The protagonist does something unrequested that changes the situation |
| Knowledge tiers | A hidden fact reaches the audience, or an audience-known fact reaches a named character |
| Relationship / power | Trust, debt, leverage, allegiance or required action shifts |
| Both plots | The external task and the secret or relationship plot each advance |
| Clocks | Every planted deadline visibly expires or is beaten; none vanishes between episodes |

Adding another event in the same state is not progress. Rewrite the responsible beat.
Track audience knowledge separately from each character's knowledge and the hidden-fact bank.

## Six ending shapes

| Shape | Last beat |
|---|---|
| Reveal | The audience gets a fact a character on screen lacks |
| Threat | Danger arrives or acts before its consequence lands |
| Decision | A person faces a choice; cut before the answer |
| Recognition | Two people see each other for what they are |
| Reversal | Power flips between people |
| Discovery | An object changes the meaning of what came before |

No consecutive repeat. No shape appears more than twice in any five episodes. Use at least four
shapes per season across episodes with successors. Check the last four accepted shapes plus the
proposed one, and check the whole proposed arc. A five-slot history of Reveal, Threat, Reveal,
Decision rules out Reveal and Decision next. Threat, Recognition, Reversal or Discovery can fit.

A short run with fewer than four successor slots cannot use four shapes. Use as much variety as
its length allows and record that limit rather than inventing episodes. A settled finale is not
a seventh cliffhanger shape. Prefer a held image with an information gap over a portentous line.

## The terminal episode

Answer the season question and close the promised open threads on screen. Pay out the reveal
bank as needed; the one-reveal ceiling and non-shrinking ledger no longer apply. Let the rupture
heal or reach its final reckoning, with its cost acknowledged. End on a settled image. Do not
open a new exit question when nobody is left to answer it. Initiative, knowledge transfer,
relationship change, both plots and honored clocks still apply.
