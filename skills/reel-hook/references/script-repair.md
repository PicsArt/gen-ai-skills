# A — Diagnose and improve a script

Preserve the user's subject, voice, story and claims unless asked to change them. If the script is already strong, make the smallest useful edit.

1. Identify the current promise, audience, first reward, payoff, and CTA. Read all supplied material before rewriting the opening.
2. Mark loss-of-interest risks by timestamp or line: delayed context, generic opening, unnecessary setup, repeated information, unsupported claim, confusing visual, premature answer with no further value, missing payoff, unrelated sales pivot, crowded CTA. A text-only diagnosis predicts risks; it does not establish actual retention drops.
3. Separate hook failure from body/offer failure. A better opening cannot fix a weak demonstration or a broken checkout. Use actual retention curves when provided; distinguish observed drop from your explanation of it.
4. Draft materially different openings: a direct proof option, a recognition/question option, and a visual option when suitable. Explain audience and production tradeoffs. Do not force a surreal concept onto sincere or sensitive material.
5. Choose one and rewrite the full sequence, so the body delivers the selected promise. Provide exact speech and actions, not just “insert hook.” Include a natural version of the CTA; sales and sharing need not both be spoken commands.
6. Check duration by spoken-word estimate and action time. State any estimated runtime. If video is available, inspect sound-on/off playback and phone-size readability before claiming audiovisual QA.

Deliver: short diagnosis → alternatives → selected full timed revision → key changes → test variable. Include before/after only where it helps the user see an important change. Apply the common hook card and rubric in SKILL.md without padding a simple task.

Example repair: “Welcome to our shop. Today we are excited to show our new organizer. It is amazing. Visit our website.” becomes a visible cable snag followed by the organizer's verified routing action, then a clear finished desk and an invitation to check its dimensions. Do not invent “saves 30 minutes” or “never tangles again.”
