# From an observed reference to a reusable opening

Use when analyzing supplied Reels/posts, adding visual library entries, or adapting a reference into a script or prompt. Scale detail to the job. A static post needs composition analysis, not invented motion or sound.

## Keep observation, interpretation and design separate

Maintain three linked records: source evidence, mechanism interpretation, and proposed adaptation. Preserve the original source record when a new design changes it. Every material observation has evidence (source moment/frame/transcript), an access method, and confidence. Unknown is a valid result, not a reason to invent precision.

Classify coverage: caption-only, sampled-playback, continuous-playback, or exact-file-inspected. Record audio separately as heard, transcribed, absent in a verified file, or unreviewed. Muted playback means unreviewed, never silent. Browser screenshots are reference evidence, not native source frames; browser video duration is not a full media probe. Sampled frames do not establish continuous motion, exact cut boundaries or absence of an event.

Use an available supported resolver for actual video when useful. A playable webpage is not a direct video URL. Inspect tool availability and pricing rather than copying another agent's calls. Bound failed retrieval attempts; keep useful partial analysis with its gaps. Native dimensions, fps, hash and audio-stream presence come from the actual file when available. Do not estimate them from the browser viewport.

## Capture the source at two levels

First describe the complete format, audience promise, opening, progression, payoff and CTA. Then break it into evidenced beats. A beat is a meaningful event, a shot is an uninterrupted camera view, and a take is a continuous recorded/generated asset; they need not map one-to-one. A compilation may contain several execution examples; a tutorial includes steps that should not be silently discarded when reproducing the tutorial.

For each beat record:

- Approximate or measured interval, purpose, subject/object IDs, initial state, action, final state; reference frames before/at/after the important event where available.
- First-frame focal point, shot size, subject scale/position, foreground/midground/background, negative space, eyeline and movement direction. Separate subject movement, camera movement and editing effects.
- Container/content aspect and crop; layers such as base footage, inset, captions, logo and UI. Distinguish visual top-to-bottom arrangement from compositing z-order. When measured, boxes use normalized x/y/width/height relative to the video content, excluding browser chrome; mark measurement basis. Unknown geometry stays null.
- Take continuity and transitions, including occlusion/contact at a transformation and state across cuts. Do not infer continuity from two matching stills alone.
- Lighting direction/source as visible, palette, contrast and depth cues. Exact camera/lens/aperture and production method remain unknown unless documented; keep proposed lens settings in the adaptation, not source facts.
- Speech, on-screen text, caption cadence/emphasis, music/foley/voice and timing only as observed. UI steps must distinguish actual changing screens from static plates.
- What the viewer knows now, what remains unresolved, when a new reward arrives, and which evidence fulfills the promise. Separate the hook's resolution from the business CTA.

## Explain triggers with events, not labels

For each relevant mechanism save its role (primary/secondary/absent/unknown), observable cue, viewer-question hypothesis, withheld outcome, escalation, resolution evidence and failure risk. Do not treat these as measured psychological effects.

| Trigger | Specific information worth collecting |
|---|---|
| Anticipation | Pending event, progress cues, range of expected outcomes, time to first reward, whether the promised outcome actually appears |
| Impossibility | Familiar physical rule apparently violated, frame that makes it legible, change in scale/support/material, reveal, product identity preserved through it |
| Danger / threatened consequence | What could be lost/harmed, visible proximity to that outcome, escape/rescue/resolution, fictional versus claimed demonstration, brand/audience fit |

Never require all three. Humor, identity, sensory reward and direct useful proof remain alternatives. Separate presence of a cue from evidence that it improved retention or sales.

## Save the reusable mechanism and the execution

Keep a stable family such as 'unsupported objects resolve into a useful arrangement', plus source-specific execution details. Tag product compatibility, audience situation, organic/paid placement, intended goal, product-bridge strength, production difficulty and reasons not to use it. Do not treat a family, variant and source repost as three unique discoveries. Keep observed popularity separate from tested performance; a winning test has an account, comparison, dates, denominator and objective.

## Translate guidance into an adaptation map

Use the user's stated goal, fidelity level, substitutions and brand rules. In this hook library, proposed product examples are adaptations unless the user asks for closer reproduction. Explicitly label that choice. For ambiguous high-fidelity production, clarify consequential changes before generation; analysis and draft alternatives can proceed.

For every retained source beat map: source event → job it performs → new product event → what stays → what changes → reason. Distinguish required user constraints from our creative preferences. Preserve the original requirements separately from the production board so model defects cannot redefine success. For close replication, check coverage of every required action/layer; for mechanism adaptation, preserve the attention-to-payoff relationship while openly changing duration, cast, words, setting or technique.

Compile prompts from these decisions: scene/setup and real assets; shot framing and subject placement; action with direction and physical result; restrained performance if relevant; light sources; speech/sound or deliberate silence; timing; transition and final state. Use continuity.md for multi-shot state tracking. Direct the action rather than adding generic quality adjectives. Keep verified product capabilities separate from fictional effects. Build variants around one declared change and a concrete test hypothesis.

Before calling a package generation-ready, check assets, model controls, physical/timing feasibility and all required beats. When an output exists, inspect it against the source requirements and declared changes, not only the prompt. Retain timestamps of failures and verify both joins after repairs. A schema-valid record does not establish visual fidelity or performance.

## Reusable commercial recipe fields

For library collection and reuse, record both the reference's triggers and the adaptation's recommended triggers, each with a role and reason. Record hook execution type separately from trigger (for example, floating-object reveal versus impossibility). Keep source sounds and proposed sound design separate; capture timed sound cues, their purpose, music, speech, ambience, deliberate silence and muted readability.

Name variable elements and invariants explicitly. Variables can include clothing, cast, setting, experience, product, service result, prop, composition, timing, sound, message and CTA. For each useful substitution, state what may change, what must remain, required real assets/proof and incompatible changes. Map applications to offer type (physical product, service or experience), audience situation, objective, product role, transition, CTA destination and metric. A romantic balloon ride, canoe outing, nail service and haircut need different product connections; do not treat every dramatic ending as a valid commercial bridge.

Store complete prompt examples with model/settings and resolved variables, plus generated samples linked to the exact prompt version and review status. Distinguish observed source entries, reusable recipe families and generated executions. Keep proposed recipes untested; never represent generated service outcomes or fictional excursions as real business evidence. Use the project's template/record index when available, rather than creating disconnected idea lists.
