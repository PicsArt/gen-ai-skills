# Visual hook planning and library composition

Use for visual-first, silent, hybrid and reference-derived Reels. This is a creative planning scheme, not an empirically validated performance formula. Text and speech can clarify the story; they are not required to create the initial question.

## The sequence: anchor → disruption → progression → bridge → payoff → action

1. **Anchor:** Give the viewer a recognizable person, object, task or setting. Specify the first-frame focal point, scale and camera position. The unusual event must be legible on a phone.
2. **Disruption:** Start a visible change or contradiction. Write the question it creates without relying on captions. Choose one dominant mechanism; secondary triggers must earn their complexity.
3. **Progression:** Show consequential change toward an answer. State direction, contact, timing and intermediate rewards. More rotations or bigger danger are not inherently better retention.
4. **Bridge:** Connect the hook to the offer through the same object, person, action, material, shape, setting or actual problem. Specify the outgoing and incoming image, motion and sound. A visual match alone does not establish commercial relevance.
5. **Payoff:** Resolve the opening and show a recognizable product, useful result or experience. Separate fictional staging from verified product/service evidence. Budget time for understanding and desire, not just spectacle.
6. **Action:** Choose one next step appropriate to the objective, in the video or permitted caption surface. A deliberate no-CTA creative piece is valid.

Write a one-sentence concept: **For [audience/goal], [recognizable situation] undergoes [visible disruption]; [progression] resolves through [motivated bridge] into [offer/payoff], inviting [action].** Then write actual timecodes for the chosen runtime; these six functions do not require six shots or fixed durations.

## Keep four decisions separate

| Decision | Examples |
|---|---|
| Trigger: why watch? | Anticipation, impossibility, threatened consequence; also humor, recognition, sensory pleasure or useful proof |
| Execution: what happens? | Floating outfit, interrupted reach, near spill, final piece fitting, haircut reveal |
| Bridge: how does the offer enter? | Same product stays in frame; hand carries it across a cut; sole occlusion; splash-to-ripple; cape wipe |
| Commercial role: why this offer? | Product is the spectacle, performer wears it, service delivers the reveal, experience is the destination, tool explains the effect |

Record anticipation, impossibility and danger separately as primary, secondary, omitted or unknown, with reasons. Do not optimize to include all three. For example, a floating outfit needs no danger; a tactile glaze reveal needs neither danger nor impossible physics.

## Compose variety from existing material

Retrieve compatible recipes by audience need, offer, objective and evidence before selecting spectacle. Vary **mechanism × product role × bridge × framing × emotional tone × payoff format**, while filtering incompatible combinations. A new shirt color or setting is an execution variant, not a new mechanism. Retain a stable family ID and source lineage.

| Existing seed | Distinct extension to propose | Product connection and test objective |
|---|---|---|
| Floating outfit / VH-006 | Floating tableware settles into breakfast; gift pieces assemble into a box | Actual collection is visible throughout; discovery versus collection visits |
| Interrupted reach / VH-004–005, salon | Hand reaches for one object, another hand completes its matching set | Gift or bundle reveal; qualified interest, not merely reactions to cash |
| Salon transformation | Chair turn, cape wipe or mirror reveal; close nail-detail reveal | Service result is the payoff; bookings require authentic service evidence |
| Coconut splash / EX-003 | Macro pour, condensation, satisfying opening; matched splash into serving | Sensory pleasure and refreshment mood; product visits versus completion |
| Plane sole / EX-006 | Ordinary step brings sole toward lens, then product detail | Keeps the shoe bridge with lower motion complexity; compare with stunt opening |
| Dam splash / EX-007 | Small droplet → glass ripple → café sip | Same refreshment payoff without the high-altitude stunt; compare qualified interest |
| Anticipation / VH-001 | Final puzzle piece, opening gift lid, cloth sliding off a product | The item is the awaited answer; consideration or saves |
| Threatened consequence / VH-003 | Hand catches a slipping cup, then shows its handle | Low-stakes relief; do not imply unverified impact resistance |

Also derive creator-facing edits from the same assets: **finished effect → actual process → repeatable lesson**. A process explanation fits saves/inquiries; the shopper edit should prioritize the product. Do not fabricate a real process from generated imagery.

For an initial six-concept exploration, consider two product-led reveals, one sensory scene, one transformation, one playful interaction and one surreal/stunt idea. This is a starting mix, not a posting quota. Adjust to brand, assets, audience and results. Prefer uncovered mechanisms over ever more extreme variants of the same stunt.

## From recipe to renderable plan

Use reference-intake.md first when rendering. Fill the asset roles and continuity locks, then compile the sequence using video-prompts.md. Keep first-frame layout, subject movement and camera movement separate. Sound cues need their own timing and purpose; use silence where useful. Check the muted story, then sound-on clarity.

Recent experiments provide production cautions, not performance proof:
- Plane: keep the shoe attached to the performer until the sole actually occludes the lens; a disconnected shoe reveal is a failed bridge.
- Dam: a prompt requesting rotations does not verify their count. Track the full action in continuous playback. Hand-supported motion is not an unsupported salto; a pause breaks an explicitly requested rebound.
- Café: connected hand → grip → lift → sip → replacement, with fixed glass/can positions and stable identity.
- Salon: low camera near the cash makes foreground motivation legible; preserve the requested actor interaction and actual before/after distinction.
- Coconut: match splash direction and preserve package shape and branding.

When choreography fails, preserve the user's requested version as the acceptance target. Propose simpler action, shorter shot generation or compositing as alternatives; do not silently redefine success or launch unlimited paid retries.

## Record, review and learn

Use the bundled assets/visual-hook-record-template.json for new records; preserve existing project schemas when extending them. Store source observations separately from planned formula beats, reference bindings and rendered results. Tag objective, trigger, execution family, bridge, product role, shot complexity, sound treatment and evidence status.

Keep statuses distinct: observed-source; proposed-untested; generated-for-review; production-reviewed; performance-tested. A production pass is not a performance win. Keep failed versions linked to exact prompts and reference manifests. Log continuous-motion and audio coverage separately, and leave unchecked criteria unknown.

Compare one declared variable while keeping the rest as stable as practical. For reach, inspect available early retention, completion and shares; for sales, inspect qualified visits, inquiries, add-to-cart or purchases as available. Record denominator, dates, audience, placement and spend; organic comparisons are confounded. Promote a recipe only for the objective supported by its evidence.
