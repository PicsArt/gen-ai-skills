# C — Catalog to hook and feed plan

Use the supplied website or connected catalog. If neither is provided, ask for the URL while preparing the planning structure; never invent a catalog. Crawl only relevant public/authorized pages and respect access restrictions.

## Build a factual product map

Read category pages and representative product detail pages, customization/options, shipping/returns, FAQs and available first-party reviews. Record coverage: categories/products inspected, URLs, date, inaccessible or excluded sections. For large catalogs, sample across price, category, use case and production complexity; explain the sampling and don't claim exhaustive analysis.

Product rows should contain SKU/name/URL, actual function, price/currency and date if relevant, dimensions/materials/options, availability status if verified, audience/job, differentiating feature, objection, supporting evidence, media assets, purchase path, and unknowns. Distinguish catalog presence from inventory. Margins, bestsellers, conversion rates and customer demographics remain unknown unless supplied.

Group by buyer need, not just taxonomy: small-space fit, gifting, maintenance, visible personalization, collection-building, practical replacement. Do not infer medical, developmental, animal-safety, food-contact or outdoor suitability from appearance. Keep those claims out unless supported.

## Prioritize and vary

Assess products qualitatively on relevance, demonstrable difference, proof/assets, buyability, production effort and commercial priority supplied by the owner. Explain rankings with facts and confidence. Unknown margin is not a high-margin score. “Best hooks” means best-supported hypotheses for this catalog, not forecast winners.

Plan across discovery, consideration and conversion:

- Discovery: recognizable situation, visual play, design inspiration, entertaining process.
- Consideration: fit, materials, real comparison, customization walkthrough, objection/FAQ.
- Conversion: exact available option, verified offer or delivery information, product-specific destination.

Use the user's cadence. Without one, propose a small pilot (for example 9 posts across 3 weeks), not an asserted optimal posting frequency. Mix product families, viewer needs, mechanisms, intensity and sales pressure. Avoid adjacent concepts with the same opening device or identical SKU unless intentionally testing a series. Keep a repeat ledger of SKU, angle, first frame and CTA. A repeatable series may retain its format while answering a different substantive question each time.

For each planned item provide: slot; product and source; audience need; funnel role; exact first shot/line; mechanism; payoff; evidence/asset requirement; production approach; share reason; CTA/destination; primary metric; priority/confidence. Identify which concepts are ready to script and which await facts/assets. Expand the top few into full hook cards rather than producing only abstract content pillars.

## Buying path

Verify the intended destination exists and matches the demonstrated product/options. Use the supported route (profile link, product tag, store page or DM) and do not promise a link automation that has not been configured. Check whether shipping region, customizer limitations or unavailable variants undermine the promise. Report site problems without changing the website unless asked.

Use one primary CTA per item; a specific social recognition can create sharing without an explicit share command. A catalog plan can combine share-led and purchase-led posts rather than requiring every Reel to do everything. No “comment a word and I will DM you” promise unless fulfillment is actually available.

## Creative pilot ideas to adapt, not assert as current trends

1. **Impossible opening, real demonstration:** surreal first beat resolves into an honest product-use sequence.
2. **Proof before polish:** a real fit/use demonstration with one strong sensory cue.
3. **One design decision per episode:** show a rejected alternative and actual reason for the chosen feature.
4. **Choose for your situation:** two valid options with a clear tradeoff, not fake superiority.
5. **Recipient recognition:** a small story a buyer wants to send to a partner or friend.
6. **Reveal the illusion:** follow a spectacle Reel with a process episode that builds trust and craft interest.

To call an idea trending, attach recent comparable examples, market/region and observation dates. New model capability is an enabler, not evidence of audience demand. Maintain an evergreen fallback if a trend is stale, poorly matched, or unavailable for commercial audio use.
