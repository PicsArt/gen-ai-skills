# Delivery: QC, masters, archive, disclosure

## QC

On the full final export, ideally by fresh eyes. Every note is closed before masters: fixed on the
final take, fixed as a text layer or a sound fix, or accepted by the user in writing.

- **Picture:** extra fingers, drifting objects, flicker, boiling textures, pseudo-text on signs and
  screens.
- **Joins:** dropped or black frames, sound out of sync at a seam.
- **Digital:** banding in gradients, over-compression.
- **Titles:** spelling, rights, required credits, and any AI-use notice a platform or festival asks
  for. Titles are text layers, never generated.
- **Sound:** level steps between scenes, clipping, a bed swallowing a line.

## Masters

| Purpose | Here |
|---|---|
| Online, streaming | Yes: `picsart_scene_export`, within its size limit |
| Vertical or square promo | Yes, as a new composition per shot, framed so faces and action survive; often a shorter selects-of-selects cut rather than the whole film in a phone frame |
| Archive master (a mastering codec at full resolution) | No. The final-resolution takes filed in the project's Drive folder are its sources |
| Cinema package, surround mix, stems | No. Hand off to a post house |

Each master is a separate export. A reframe is a composition of its own: check its framing shot
by shot.

## Handoff package

When a colourist or a sound team takes over: the clean cut, each shot's source file at final
quality, the shot order and windows from the record, the look (tone and colour), the sound
references, and the takes' audio as it is. It is already in the project folder; the package is the
list.

## The archive

Everything a later session needs to regenerate a broken shot, recut a scene or start a sequel with
the same faces. It is filed in the project's Drive folder and kept in the project record. Filing
is not a guarantee that a file stays available, so keep the record current:

- the final prompts, every prompt version and their tokens, and the generation log;
- the asset rows: descriptors, voice lines, sheets and views;
- the story, the shot list and the look;
- the cut: shot order, windows and transitions per scene;
- every select at final resolution, and the masters;
- the rights notes: voices, the music's licence status, the disclosure text.

The archive is the means of production, not a keepsake.

## Disclosure

Festivals and platforms increasingly ask which AI tools and models made a film, and some disqualify
an incomplete answer. Write it mechanically from the record, not from memory:

- every model used, per stage: pictures, video, edits and extends, upscaling, music;
- a statement of what people contributed (the story, the choices, the edit);
- an AI credits block for the titles.

Give it to the user as text, and keep it in the record. It costs nothing, and the film cannot be
submitted without it.
