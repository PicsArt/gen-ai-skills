# What a short cut is

The one place that says what a short cut from longer footage is, and how to judge one. Every step
that picks, challenges or judges a cut uses it: the moment picker, the challenger, the critics and
the cold watch. It adds no tool and no step; it is how to think while doing the ones you have.

## The unit is one question, answered on screen

A short is one question the viewer holds, and the footage answering it. It is not a quote. A line
someone said is material you use inside the unit, never the unit itself.

Why it matters: in long footage of action (a game, a sport, a build), the best-phrased lines are
usually said after the action, summing it up. Pick by the line and you pick the review instead of
the thing reviewed, and the short tells the viewer that something happened instead of showing it.

The same unit, seen other ways. Each one gives you the edges:

- **An event**: something happens, with a before and an after. The edges fall on its cause and
  its consequence.
- **A question opened and closed**: "Is that drone friendly?" then "Nope." The in-point is where
  the question becomes clear; the out-point is the answer plus one beat of reaction.
- **A change of state**: alive to dead, confident to humbled. The cut holds both states.
- **A claim and its proof**: a boast belongs only if the footage proves it inside the short.
- **The title's promise**: the title is a contract, and the cut is whatever pays it.

## Cut for the cold viewer

The viewer does not know the game, the people, the show or the stream, and owes you nothing. Cut
for them. The others are limits on that cut, not audiences of their own:

- **the creator**: the cut is true, and something they would post;
- **the brief**: it fits the format asked for;
- **the feed**: it is only many cold viewers, so never model it apart from them.

Whoever has read the whole transcript cuts from the creator's side without noticing: they know
who everyone is and what came before. Forget it on purpose, and judge only what the cut shows.

## What makes a cut right

- By about second two, the viewer holds one specific question.
- Everything after it raises the stakes of that question or answers it.
- It ends as soon as the question is answered and the answer is felt.
- The voice frames, the picture proves, the face shows the feeling. When the voice claims what the
  picture does not prove, the cut is wrong, however good the line. What counts as proof depends on
  the footage (below).
- It ends on its peak: the strongest line or the event's last beat, then one beat of reaction and
  out. Nothing after it softens it.

## What counts as proof, per kind of footage

| Kind | The moment is | The proof is |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** (a game, a sport, a build) | an event, from its cause to its consequence, closed by the reaction | the picture. A spoken claim the picture does not show is cut. |
| **Conversation** (a podcast, an interview) | a claim and its proof, or a story with its turn | the speaker's own story, number, name or example, said inside the cut. The face is the picture. A boast with no proof inside the cut is cut. |
| **Talk** (a lecture, a keynote, a lesson) | one idea with its example | the example said inside the cut, the slide, or the room's laugh. |

Every moment carries three fields, so these questions are asked when the pool is made, not after:

- `kind`: `action`, `conversation` or `talk`;
- `question`: what the viewer holds by second two, in the viewer's words;
- `answer_at`: the second of the source where it is answered, inside the moment's range.

A moment with no `question`, or whose `answer_at` falls outside its range, is not offered.

## One question, and the loops

One question is the unit. The retention-hooks skill's intensifier sharpens it; a rehook is only for
a clip whose answer raises a second question. A clip that has answered its question is over,
whatever the target length.

**The opener may show that something happens, never how or why.** That is the question. A line
moved to the front to open the clip comes from the same answer or the same event (both ranges
inside one moment), runs under about two seconds, and could not mislead a viewer about what it
responds to. A line from another situation is never moved (question 6).

## Eight questions for any candidate

1. **Question**: what does the viewer hold by second two? Say it in the viewer's words.
2. **Answer**: is it answered on screen, in this cut? Where, to the second?
3. **Proof**: where is the proof of every spoken claim, by the kind of footage (above)? A claim
   with none: cut the line.
4. **Stranger**: would someone new to this game, show or person follow it?
5. **Title**: is the title that question, and does the cut pay it, literally?
6. **One event**: does everything come from one continuous situation? If not, would the creator
   call the joined version honest?
7. **Edges**: does it start when the question appears and stop when it is answered? What is each
   second outside that for?
8. **The set**: is it a different kind of story from the other shorts in the same delivery? The
   kinds: a hot take, a story, a how-to, a number, a laugh or reaction, a reveal.

## What these catch

- **Telling, not showing**: a short spoke four claims and its picture proved one.
- **Stitching across events**: lines from minutes apart, reordered into one fight, put a line from
  another situation on the wrong moment. That is untrue, not only weak craft (question 6).
- **One story told four times**: each short of a set was judged alone, and nobody judged the set
  (question 8).
- **The source's own premise left unused**: a running joke or thread the whole video is named for,
  never used by any short.
- **What worked**: single-take shorts built on one question, or on a reversal ("I've never had a
  run like this" then "I'm dead").
- **The unpaid tease**: a clip that promotes the full video still answers its own question. Its
  last line may then open a larger one that only the full video answers. A short that withholds the
  answer it promised is bait.

## A worked example

A 38 s supercut joined five ranges of a stream. It became one continuous take of about 20 s:

- the question: out of bullets, the enemy right there: does the reload get off?
- it opens on "I'm out of bullets" and ends on the kill and the reaction;
- the title is that question;
- the boasts the footage never proved were dropped.

It was diagnosed from the transcript alone, so its "proven" calls were inference. Check them on the
frames before you rely on them.

## Sentence edges

Clean sentence edges stay the rule for never clipping a word (the speech-aware-cutting skill). They
are not what a moment is. The question and its answer decide where the cut starts and stops; the
transcript then sets the exact frame of each edge.
