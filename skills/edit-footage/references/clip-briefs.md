# Campaign briefs

Clippers earn on campaigns: a creator or brand posts a brief on a clipping platform (Whop,
Discord servers, agencies) saying what to clip from their content, what to avoid and what every
post must carry. When the user pastes one, or links the source episode from one, the clips are
cut to the brief. Never go looking for campaigns, trending videos or other creators' content to
clip: the user brings the brief and the source.

## Split the brief three ways

**1. The selection rules**, into the record's `brief.read`. Only what changes which moments are
chosen or how they are described:

- who and what the source is: the show, the guest, the episode;
- approved clip types, in the brief's own priority order;
- prohibitions: banned topics, people, footage, framings. Keep every one as a "Never" line;
  nothing else in the pipeline can enforce them;
- named clip ideas or example moments, one line each with the angle to lead on;
- accuracy rules: no misquoting, no out-of-context cuts.

Write them as second-person imperative lines, one rule per line, prohibitions last. Never invent
a rule the brief does not state and never soften one it does. Do not restate what the job already
does: complete thoughts, clean in and out points, strong hooks, skipping intros and sponsor
reads, not reusing a moment.

**2. The numbers**, into `brief.assumed` as stated values: the clip length as a range ("30 to
60 seconds" is both bounds), the clip count, the source link.

**3. What the cut cannot enforce**: mandatory caption lines, required @handles, required
hashtags, on-screen branding. Keep them as a list, copied character for character, @ signs and
capitalisation included. They go into every clip's post copy at delivery, and each one is
checked there. On-screen branding the clips cannot place is said so once.

Drop everything nothing downstream reads: payouts, CPM, budgets, deadlines, view quotas, account
rules, Discord and support links.

## Platforms and ratio

The brief names where the clips get posted. Vertical feeds (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) mean 9:16. A
YouTube-only campaign that wants long-form uploads means 16:9 at 1920×1080: the canvas, the
reframe and the export all take it, and the look's layout is fitted to it. Say which you picked, and why, in
the plan message.

## Photo campaigns

A brief that asks for photos is not this job. Say the clips are video and leave its rules
unapplied.

## Before delivery

Read every clip's post copy against the list from step 3. A missing line is added, not
mentioned. A clip that breaks a prohibition in the brief is dropped, and why is said in one line.
