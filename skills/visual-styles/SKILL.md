# Visual styles

A style is a set of choices held across a whole scene: a small palette with one reserved hero colour, how its
shapes are built, how time is stepped, one typeface and a frame checklist. Pick the style first, take its
reference and keep to it; mixing two styles in one scene reads as a mistake. Every reference was built as shapes
and text in one scene and rendered and checked frame by frame; the lines in them are fragments to adapt, not
ready-to-run calls.

Structure adapted from cth9191/animate (MIT); the content is our own.

## Build a styled film with the MCP tools

1. Pick the style (the picker, or the table above) and fetch its reference. Say which one you picked.
2. Draw the cast and the place in that style's line, fill and palette. A library character keeps its rig and
   actions but is restyled as a new item; the house look is never pasted onto a styled film. Character and place
   share the outline weight, the palette and the shade rule.
3. Build the scene as JSON and write it with the scene tools; check it with `picsart_scene_validate`, preview with
   and look across the whole clip with `picsart_scene_contact_sheet`.
4. A script may compute numbers (a path's vertices, a table of offsets, beat times); the scene itself is built
   and edited only through the tools.
5. Run [FRAME.md](references/FRAME.md) and the style's own checklist, then its originality guard, before showing it.

## Rules every style shares

- **Originality.** Each style is a way of drawing, not a franchise. Draw your own characters, props and
  compositions: no look-alike of a famous character, no copy of a famous artwork's composition, no named
  studio's logo, title card or catch phrase. A character is original when its silhouette, costume and face are
  invented; a "tribute" is not.
- **Stepping is a nested composition.** To animate on twos or fours, put the pieces in a `composition` layer
  whose `composition.fps` is 12 (twos) or 6 (fours) and key them with plain keys: the nested rate steps them.
  Do not write time-floor expressions. Pieces that share a rate share one nested composition so they stay in step.
- **Jitter and boil are presets.** Hand jitter and tremble are `wiggle` (give neighbouring parts different
  seeds), a redrawn outline is `line_boil`, a held-then-jumping value is `random_hold`; apply them with
  `picsart_scene_apply_preset` to the layer ids inside the nested composition.
- **Cued layers use `timeMode: "layer"`.** A layer that starts after 0 and has keys of its own counts them from
  its own start. A composition layer runs on its own local clock: write local times and check none lands past the
  composition's end.
- **A look over the whole picture needs the picture as an asset.** A look (grain, film finish) applies to media
  and `scene_ref` layers, not a shape layer: put the drawn scene in `assets` as a `scene` asset and place it once
  with a `scene_ref` layer carrying the `looks`.
- **Painted texture is the paint's `brush`.** Gouache streaks inside a fill, dry-brush lettering and textured
  ink lines are a `brush` on the `fill`, `stroke` or text content (see
  the `scene-animation-reference` skill, `references/brushed-paint.md`), not a texture image or a stack of hand-drawn streak
  shapes. Give neighbouring fills different `seed`s and angles so the streaks do not line up.
- **A scaled group scales its stroke.** An iris, vignette or ring whose line must keep its width animates the
  ellipse's own `size`, never the group's `scale`.
- **`mask.clip` takes shape content** as well as a colour rectangle: clip a glare or a tone to a circle by giving
  the clip shape-layer content.
- **Inside a shape layer an earlier item is on top.** List highlights, cut-outs and details first and the large
  mass last; across layers a later layer is on top.
- **One repeater per group.** A grid of dots or panes is a row group (item plus repeater) inside a column group.
- **Easing is an object.** `{"kind": "cubic_bezier", "control": [x1, y1, x2, y2]}`; a snap is `[0.7, 0, 0.15, 1]`,
  a small overshoot `[0.3, 1.5, 0.5, 1]`; a hard cut is the `step` easing.
- **A parent layer with nothing to draw still needs a `transform`.** With none it sits at the frame centre and
  moves its children there.
- **Taste, for every style.** One hero per shot, the rest quieter. A rare effect (a glitch, a light sweep, a
  stamp, a camera move) has a per-film budget: spend it once, on the idea that earns it. Open on a concrete fact or
  a question, not a title. A route or an arrow always connects two things, from and to. Captions and HUD stay
  outside a camera move so they never jump at a cut. For a song or a beat: changes land on bar lines, hits on
  beats; song sections become scenes, and the chorus returns to its set and escalates.
  For a motion style: vary the transition kind cut to cut (never the same one three times running); add sound only
  where something happens.
- **Check the whole clip.** Frames across the window, never one still: `picsart_scene_contact_sheet`, then the motion checks
  in the `quality-review` recipe.
