# Animation

Preserve the approved medium and style. Camera language can apply to animation, especially 3D or deliberate photographic treatments; do not impose a lens or remove one solely because the work is animated.

## Drawing and rendering

Specify relevant choices: line colour and weight, fill treatment, shading steps, material, palette, and the relation between characters and backgrounds. Black outlines, painted interiors, depth of field and lens effects can all be intentional. Do not assign them universally to a technique or forbid them by default.

Use approved style and character references. Preserve proportions, eye construction, wardrobe and silhouette across independent generations, with required descriptors carried into each call. Repetition supports consistency but does not prove it.

## Timing and performance

Name a held pose, anticipation, follow-through, overshoot/settle or a smear only when it serves the action and style. On ones requests a new drawing each frame; on twos requests a drawing held for two frames. A prompt does not guarantee actual frame cadence; verify output frames or use appropriate animation/editing controls.

A flat layered scene can use different layer speeds for parallax; a 3D scene can use perspective camera travel. Keep camera motion and drawing changes distinct. A still held pose can be correct; morphing is not evidence that the prompt merely forgot a hold.

## Examples and checks

- “Keep the approved black outlines and flat fills; hold the pose, then turn the head toward the door.”
- “The coat follows the body turn and settles after the stop.”
- “Pan across the layered background with nearer layers crossing faster than distant layers.”

These are untested instructions. Review line consistency, face identity, pose transitions, contact and timing through the full take. Do not change the number of characters or the intended style as an unannounced defect workaround.
