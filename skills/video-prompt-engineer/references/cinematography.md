# Cinematography

Treat craft language as a way to express visible intent, not proof of how a generator was trained or a guarantee that it will obey.

Choose movement for the scene's purpose: a push can concentrate attention, a pull-back can reveal surroundings, and an arc can reveal shape. These are possible uses, not universal emotional meanings. A stationary shot or still subject is valid. A coordinated arc and rise is not inherently wrong; state the route, aim, timing and ending.

Read [camera](camera.md), [lighting and colour](light-and-colour.md) and the relevant [craft refinements](craft-refinements.md). Describe field of view, distance and focus rather than claiming a lens number determines perspective or a complete optical simulation.

For a sequence, map geography, references and action phase. Preserve a light's location in the scene; its apparent screen side can change in reverse coverage. Establishing views, inserts, reactions and close-ups serve different purposes. Wide-medium-close is one possible progression, not a mandatory order.

Repeated descriptors support continuity, but references and review of generated takes remain necessary. Diagnose unstable geometry, composition, motion, light and edit joins separately. Do not attribute every problem to an unnamed camera move.
