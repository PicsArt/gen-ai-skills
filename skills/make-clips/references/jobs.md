# The jobs

The footage and the request decide the job. Say it back in one line, and
keep it in the record's `job`. Never ask it. Anything the user says wins over a job's default.

| Job | Footage | Moments picked by | Output | Music | Captions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `podcast-clips` | a long podcast or interview, one to three speakers | the transcript and the stills, each moment ranked to stand on its own | several shorts (three unless the user names a number), no moment used twice, the speaker kept in frame | none | word-timed, on |
| `split` | a podcast or interview with two people on screen, and a split-screen reference (often the channel's own) | as `podcast-clips` | as `podcast-clips`, each short two stacked halves with full-frame moments, as the format sets: the footage-reframing skill's split layout | none | word cards on the seam, and the hook card, as the format sets |
| `channel-clips` | one long video, as a file or a link from elsewhere, with a short whose look the clips take (the user's own channel's, else any reel) | as `podcast-clips`; a campaign brief's rules first | several clips (three unless the user or a brief names a number), each in the look: the layout, the hook card, the captions and the end card the short has, with a cover and post copy, in one Drive folder; `references/channel-clips.md` | none | as the look sets |

## The record's stages

The record's `pipeline` is the same for every job, in the core order: `intake, reference, log,
story, plan, build, qc, critics, review, deliver`. `podcast-clips` keeps `reference` only when a
reference short or the user's own channel was given, known before the record starts; `split`
always has one, and so has `channel-clips`: the look is the job.

## The defaults, never asked

Each is set from the user's words when they say it, else from a campaign brief
(`references/clip-briefs.md`), else from the default here, and kept in `brief.assumed`. None is a
question: the step that needs it says what it decided in one line.

| What | The default | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| Destination | 9:16 at 1080×1920 and 30 fps, for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, words kept clear of all three apps' buttons (`references/platforms.md`) | a platform or a shape the user names; a look's platform for `channel-clips` |
| Goal | grow the audience: the moment a stranger would stop for, cut to stand on its own | "promote the episode" (a tease that leaves the best question open, ends on where to watch), "sell" (the moment that proves the claim, ends on the speaker's own ask), "show the speaker" (their strongest line per clip) |
| Audience | new to the topic: the setup line stays in, so a stranger follows | a channel's own followers, or people in the field: open on the sharpest line, keep the jargon |
| Length | 30 to 60 s per clip, the platform's range when it names one; the sentence wins over the length | a length the user, the look or a brief names |
| How many | three, no moment used twice; fewer when the source holds fewer moments worth a clip | a number the user or a brief names |

No job asks a message or an ending: each clip carries its own message, and ends on a closed
thought.

## The default look

With no reference: a single frame on the speaker, word-timed captions low in the safe band, the
opening line on a hook card, the hook line highlighted, no end card. A `channel-clips` run with no
reference takes the `reel-structure` skill's clean default, which is the same.

## Per job

- **`channel-clips`**: the look is the job, so the reference is read before anything is picked.
  Express pace throughout. Its steps are `references/channel-clips.md`: the look asked for only
  when none is known, every clip built in the look, all of them checked and reviewed together,
  then delivered with covers and post copy, and the offer to post them.
