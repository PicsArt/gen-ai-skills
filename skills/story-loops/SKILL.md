# Story loops

Hook, body and payoff tell you where things go. They do not keep anyone watching. What keeps a
viewer is an **open loop**: a question the video has raised and not yet answered. A short holds as
long as one loop is open, and sags wherever none is.

A loop is two moments: the line that **opens** a question and the later one that **closes** it.
Never open a loop the video cannot close, and never close one before the next is open.

## The three loops

| Loop | Opens | Where | It works when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | the first question: a result, a contrast, a problem, a claim the viewer wants proved | in the first 3 s | the viewer can say what they are waiting to find out |
| **Intensifier** | the same question, sharper: a stake, a number, a "and it gets stranger", the reason it is harder than it looks | before 15 s, and inside the first quarter of a short under 60 s | it builds on the hook's question instead of starting another |
| **Rehook** | a new question, just as the first ones start to fade | near the middle, from 35% to 60% of the length | it is new: a second problem, a twist, the part the viewer did not expect, never the hook said again |

The **payoff** closes the hook's loop, at or near the end. The rehook's loop closes before or with
it.

Scale them to the length:

- **Under 20 s:** the hook and the intensifier. There is no middle to sag; the payoff comes at once.
- **20–30 s:** all three when the material has a second question; otherwise the hook and the
  intensifier, and say so.
- **Over 30 s:** all three. Without a rehook the middle loses the viewer.

## Choosing them from footage

When the lines come from what people said (a reel, a short, highlights), the loops are chosen, not
written. From the pool of moments:

- **Hook:** the sentence that raises the question the rest answers: a surprising result, a strong
  claim, a problem said plainly. A moment the watch rated `hook` is a candidate; it must still open
  a question, not just sound strong.
- **Intensifier:** a sentence right after it that raises the stakes of the same question.
- **Rehook:** a sentence that turns: "but", "except", "what nobody tells you", a second problem.
  Place it at the middle even when the speaker said it earlier, as long as it stands on its own.
- **Payoff:** the sentence that answers the hook. **Never use the payoff as the hook**: it closes
  the loop before it opens. Lead with the question, end with the answer.

Clean sentence edges still come first. A loop never justifies a clip cut mid-sentence.

## Writing them

When the lines are written, for the screen or for a voice:

- **Hook:** show the result and hold back how it was made, so the rest of the video answers
  "how?".
- **Intensifier:** the first line after the hook makes the question sharper, with a stake.
- **Rehook:** the line nearest the middle opens what comes next instead of describing what was:
  the harder request, the step the viewer did not expect.
- **Payoff:** the last result answers the hook. The call to action comes after it, never instead.

The writing's own rules still hold: no hype words, nothing the footage does not show.

## Check the loops

Before the plan goes to the user, list each loop in one line: what it opens, the second it opens,
the second it closes. Then read it back:

- Does a loop open in the first 3 s?
- Is some loop open at every second until the payoff, or is there a stretch where the viewer has
  nothing left to wait for?
- Does every loop close?

A missing loop is a note, not a block: when the material has no second question, build without a
rehook and say so in one line.
