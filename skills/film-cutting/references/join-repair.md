# Join repair: diagnose the pair before choosing the operation

A join repair is not automatically a transition, an extend or a generation. The accepted A → B cut
stays exactly as it is while the pair is diagnosed and candidates are compared.

## Evidence

Resolve the exact source files and current windows of both sides. Look at real frames around the
boundary: take them with `picsart_scene_contact_sheet` and open them (picsart-video-rules, Asset URLs),
with enough of the action either side to follow the exchange, and say what you observed, with
source times:

- the movement's phase and direction; position, framing and eyelines;
- props, dialogue, room tone, emotional continuity;
- whether the incoming action is motivated by the outgoing beat;
- whether a black, frozen, flashing or repeated frame is in the source, in the join, or only in the
  player.

Name the failure plainly: overlap or dead time, a badly placed cut, an interrupted line,
incompatible staging, or missing story (an arrival nobody saw). Keep what you saw apart from what
you suspect. If you could not see something, say so; never guess. A deliberately abrupt cut is not
a defect.

## The smallest operation that fits

Offer only what the evidence supports, smallest first:

1. **Trim or move the cut**, for overlap, dead time or a bad cut point. Keep whole words and needed
   action.
2. **A J or L cut of the existing sound**, for a gap or an abrupt answer, where the speaker's
   visibility, the lip-sync and the meaning allow. Never duplicate or invent a line.
3. **A crop or reframe**, only if the resolution and framing allow and the subject stays in frame.
   It cannot show an arrival nobody filmed.
4. **Existing coverage, or a motivated insert**, for a missing cause, arrival, reaction or answer.
   Use coverage you have before proposing a new shot with a clear story purpose.
5. **An edit or an extend**, only when the footage supports the connection.
6. **A re-roll**, only when nothing existing can carry the connection. Say what accepted footage it
   puts at risk.

Never a dissolve, a zoom or a fade as a generic cure for positions that disagree. Honour an
explicit "crop only", and say if a crop cannot solve what you saw. **Diagnosis is not permission to
spend.**

## Designing an insert

Write a short join note: both sides' files and windows, what is wrong, the chosen repair, what must
stay untouched, and the beats outgoing → connecting → incoming. For a new shot, define both of its
edges first: where people are and what they look at, what moves, how the emotion carries, the exact
line with its speaker and who they speak to, and what motivates the next cut.

Establish an arrival before the person arriving is answered. Never replace approved speech with
silence to make a voice easier to match. A frame from the outgoing side is used only to continue a
particular pose, state or movement. The insert, extending A and re-rolling B are three different
operations; say which you propose. Each is compiled, verified, quoted and bought as film-shot-generation
says.

## Comparing

Keep every original file, window and the accepted timeline intact, and store each candidate apart,
with its source versions and settings. Compare A → B against A → candidate → B, with sound and the
lead-in and lead-out, on the real boundary frames. Never show a bridge on its own.

Open the comparison in the scene editor as its own scene: labelled sequences, distinct clip ids,
titled as a comparison. Its reply picks a candidate; it is never applied to the film as an edit.

After the user chooses, apply that candidate to the cut, check it in the whole film, and keep the
previous cut recoverable. A rejected candidate never becomes a select. Never chain paid attempts:
explain what failed and propose something materially different with its quote, or stop and keep the
original.
