# The story: ten lines, then every scene as what we see and hear

One document, written after the look is locked and to its genre. It is the one thing the user
reads, so no later shot or prompt may add a fact, motive, relationship or outcome the story never
wrote down.

## Contents

- The ten lines
- The scenes
- Playable action
- What the middle is made of
- Building to the peak
- Read it as a stranger before showing it

## The ten lines

```text
Who        the one or two people the film is with, one line each
Where      the place, and the time of day: one place unless the story needs two
Wants      what each of them wants
Against    what stands in the way: the other person, the heat, the clock, and what they fear
           about themselves
Happens    three or four beats, one line each, in order
Peak       which beat the film is built toward, and what changes there
Ends       the last thing we see
Says       the one thing the viewer is left with, in one sentence
Fixed      what the user's brief fixed (events, ending, facts), quoted, never assumed
Genre      the look's genre, in one word, and what that means for the beats
```

**Fixed** comes from the brief the user actually gave. A required event is never changed to
satisfy a line. Enrich only what the brief leaves open, and say so ("assumed: the pump is on a
concrete square") so an assumption can be corrected before it becomes canon. Read the genre line
back before the scenes: it is what stops a drama being written under an action look.

## The scenes

One paragraph per scene (two for a long one), in order, in the viewer's words:

- The first sentence says where we are and the time of day, and from the second scene on, what
  tells the viewer this is the same place or a new one.
- Then what happens, one action at a time, in the playable shape below. Lines in quotes, said by
  name. The last sentence is an action, not a line.
- The scene's length in seconds closes it, sized from its words (about 2.5 words a second, plus a
  second of silence after each line) and its actions.
- **No camera words** (close-up, wide, pan), **no film vocabulary** (beat, payoff, cold open),
  **no production notes**.
- **If a fact matters, it is on screen in the paragraph.** The water is seen, not asserted; who
  holds it is visible, not implied.
- A scene is one place at one time of day. The story changes place only where it needs to, never
  to give a video somewhere to end.

Illustrative only:

```text
Who        Rasa, who owns a flask with one swallow left. Nen, who has none.
Where      a dead standpipe on a concrete square, noon, white dust.
Wants      Rasa: to keep walking. Nen: the water.
Against    each other; two days of nothing in every direction.
Happens    Rasa checks her flask and Nen sees the water. Nen runs and hits her.
           They fight over the strap into the shade and down onto the concrete.
           Nen smashes the flask on the pump and the water runs into the dust.
Peak       the smash: the thing they are both fighting for hits the dust.
Ends       two specks on an empty plain.
Says       two people can want the same thing badly enough to destroy it.
Fixed      "two girls fighting to get a last drop of water" · it spills · 90 s
Genre      action: the fight is on the page, a charge, a throw, a fall, a smash.

Scene 2 — the square, noon, a few steps from the pump, right after scene 1.
Rasa stops and tips her flask over her palm; the last of the water spreads across
her hand. She looks at it, does not drink, and caps the flask. Nen has been watching
from the pump. "That's water." She drops her empty cup, runs across the square and
goes straight for the flask. Rasa clamps both hands on it. Nen slams into her
shoulder-first and Rasa's feet leave the ground. They hit the concrete in the shade
of the wall, both still holding the flask. About 30 seconds.
```

**A change is a rewritten paragraph**, shown whole, and the yes comes on the new text.

## Playable action

Write every action that matters in one shape:

```text
starting condition -> subject + precise verb + target or path
  -> resistance or contact -> visible reaction -> resulting condition
```

"Walks cinematically", "looks emotional" and "takes in the room" are blanks the model fills with
drift. An inner change needs something that photographs. A small action is playable when hands,
concealment, attention, contact and result all show. A fight is the same shape, one big clear
action per shot, never a flurry.

**The first scene opens on what a synopsis cannot give a viewer**: who owns our attention, where
they are, what they are doing right now, what they want, what pressure has begun, through
behaviour, in the first seconds. Never an empty room, never a pose. **The thing the story is about
is seen in the first scene**: a film about water shows water before anyone speaks of it.

## What the middle is made of

These shape the story; they are the writer's craft, never questions for the user. They come from
work on commissioned films about real lives, so they are adapted here to a short invented from an
idea, and they never bend what **Fixed** holds.

- **What they fear about themselves.** Two faces and one room push every conflict outward, toward
  what a camera can see. `Against` holds one thing inside the person as well: what they fear about
  themselves ("that he is the one who always leaves"). It is never said as a diagnosis. It is
  played: a beat where the person stops at the edge of the thing they fear, in the playable shape.
- **Two decisions.** Where the brief leaves the events open, the `Happens` beats follow one spine:
  a first decision made from pride or an assumption; what it costs; a low point where what they
  wanted turns out not to be what they needed; a second decision that undoes the first. In three
  or four beats the low point and the second decision may be one beat. Each decision is an action
  a camera can see: he reaches for his coat; he hangs it back up. A fight over water can run on its
  outer conflict alone; a film whose `Says` is about a person changing needs both decisions.

## Building to the peak

When the film is meant to move the viewer, four things on screen carry it:

1. **An early beat that makes us like the person**: a small kindness, a moment they are exposed,
   something funny about them. In the first scene, shown, not said.
2. **The peak arrives in one turn**, not by degrees. Tension builds, then releases at once. It may
   be seen coming; it must not drift in, and it must not come from nowhere.
3. **At the peak, people come together on screen**: an embrace, a hand on a shoulder, hands
   working in time.
4. **A reunion or a redemption** carries it most reliably: people apart who come back together, or
   a wrong put right. Reach for one when the brief leaves the arc open.

**The peak is played openly; every other face holds its feeling in.** Held-in feeling is the
default, because the camera is close enough to see what is held back. At the one shot the `Peak`
line names, the face that is moved shows it fully: the tears arriving, the laugh that gets away,
the shiver. We are moved by watching someone be moved, so a held-in face at the peak empties it.

- On that shot's card, the feeling says it is unguarded and names what shows ("@teo relief,
  unguarded: the tears come"). Every other card's feeling is held in, or open where the action
  opens it, and never unguarded.
- Compiled, that is the peak performance's `emotion` at intensity 3, with no `mask`, and what shows
  written in its `note`. Every other face is at 1, or 2 where the action opens it.
- One shot, not a scene. Two peaks are none.

## Read it as a stranger before showing it

Fix what you find in the text, not in a note to the user:

- Is the thing everyone wants seen before anyone speaks of it?
- Would a viewer know who has it, and where the people are, at every moment?
- Does every scene's first sentence say how we got here?
- Does the genre line match what happens?
- Is the peak one shot, with a face that plays it openly, and is every other face held in?
- Is anything in **Fixed** distorted?
- Does anything belong to another time or world than **Where** says (a phone in 1962)? A wrong
  object here becomes a scene picture, then a run.
- Is there a scene that only explains, or whose seconds hold no action? Cut it or fold it into its
  neighbour.

The one exception: where a fix would touch **Fixed**, leave the text and say it in one line under
the story. That is the user's call.
