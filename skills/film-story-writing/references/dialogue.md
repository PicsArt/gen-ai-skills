# Writing for the medium: what a line and a scene cost

Everything the film makes consistent is an asset that has to be designed, pictured, reviewed and
locked before its first scene can be shot. The story's words order those assets, so the writing
carries the budget.

## The budget is identities, not minutes

- A short comfortably affords **two or three faces and one to three rooms**. One more minute is
  nearly free; one more character is a sheet, a review and a two-shot with everyone they share a
  frame with.
- Propose the story inside a stated budget ("this fits two faces and two rooms"). Infer it from
  the idea; never ask the user how many characters they can afford.
- A named extra who acts is a character. A crowd is one asset with a count. A photograph of a dead
  relative on the wall is a prop, and possibly a face: notice what a sentence orders.

## Write what the models are good at

Faces, hands, objects and contained rooms: close performance is the medium's home. So is action,
written one big clear movement per shot.

**Spoken performance is home too.** The film's model is chosen for native audio with lip-synced
dialogue, so a talking character is not a cost and lip-sync is not a risk. Write the conversation
the story wants. Never propose a mute character, a voice-over or a "visual-only" scene to dodge
lip-sync; a scene is silent only because it plays better silent. The real audio risk is further
downstream: a voice can drift between takes, which film-cast-design's voice lines and film-shot-generation' Voice
handle.

**In-frame text is written around**, as film-shot-generation says.

## Scenes stay put

One place and one time of day per scene. A place hop mid-scene, or dusk falling inside one, orders
another set of pictures (the street by day and the street by night are two assets). A story that
returns to the same two spaces photographs better and costs half as much as one that tours five.

## Beats must photograph

A beat is carried by what the camera sees change: a prop handed over, a coat taken off, weather,
damage that stays. Each visible change is a state asset ordered by a sentence, so write them
deliberately and sparingly. An inner beat with nothing visible will not survive generation: "looks
emotional" is not a beat; a hand closing on empty coins is.

## Dialogue

- **Dialogue is action.** Before polishing a line, block the scene as action: each speaker's
  objective, what each conceals, the tactic each line performs, what is revealed or withheld and
  the behaviour it produces.
- Cut, or rewrite as behaviour: exposition both speakers know, a character naming their own
  feeling, a line that performs no action, a reveal with no reaction, a line longer than its shot.
- **Short lines, one line per shot.** A long speech breaks into shots. Budget about 2.5 words a
  second, start the line within the first 60% of its shot, and end it at least a second and a half
  before the cut, which is its beat of silence: a word the generation cuts off cannot be put back
  in the edit.
- A reaction shot is nearly free, so an exchange often plays best as line → reaction.
- **End a beat on action, not speech**: an action gives the editor a cut point and the next shot a
  hand-off.

## Words that flow downhill

The story's words become descriptors and prompts, so the language rules start in the first draft:

- never a character's age as a number; carry it through role, clothes and condition;
- actions in positive form: what happens, not what does not;
- the look belongs to the setup; the story never restates palette, stock or lens;
- design rhymes with character: a scar, a fabric, the state of the shoes restate who the person is.
  A detail that says nothing is a detail the model varies freely.

## Runtime

Count runtime in shots, not pages. A beat is usually one to three shots, each as long as film-shot-generation'
duration rule allows. No parallel
storylines on a short: cross-cutting multiplies worlds and assets faster than meaning.

## A script the user brings

Keep it: it is the story. Read it once for what the medium cannot do (in-frame text, an age as a
number) and say each in one line as a suggestion. Never soften it to protect a render.
